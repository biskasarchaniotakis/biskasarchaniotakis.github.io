CHAPTER I

The Forgotten Shell

1:01 And after the creation of the digital world, the mortals found themselves surrounded by machines whose inner workings remained hidden from their eyes.

1:02 And they desired a means to speak unto the machine, not through the painted windows of the common interface, but through the language of its own foundations.

1:03 And there existed an ancient instrument known as the Bourne Shell, called sh by the scribes of the command line.

1:04 And through it, the mortals issued commands, joined their instructions, and commanded the machine to perform their will.

1:05 Yet the ancient tongue was not without limitation, and the mortals desired a greater language.

1:06 And among the craftsmen of GNU, Brian Fox took up the ancient language and forged a new shell from the wisdom of the Bourne.

1:07 And it was called BASH, the Bourne Again Shell.

1:08 And Linus welcomed this new tongue into the Kingdom of the Kernel, and the mortals rejoiced, for their commands had found a greater home.

1:09 And the ancient shell was reborn, its language refined, its powers expanded, and its descendants spread across the machines of the Earth.

1:10 And thus was the path to the sacred Terminal opened.

"For the machine shall understand the language of those who dare to speak unto it."

CHAPTER II

The Sacred Tongue

2:01 And the Terminal was revealed unto the people, and they saw that it was unlike the ordinary windows through which they had gazed upon the digital world.

2:02 For it possessed no painted icons, no shining buttons, nor comforting promises of simplicity.

2:03 It possessed only a cursor, a command, and the patience to await the mortal's instruction.

2:04 And the wise discovered that through the Terminal they might traverse directories, summon processes, examine the workings of the machine, and shape the world through their own words.

2:05 And they learned the sacred commands, and the machine answered them.

ls To behold the contents of a directory.
cd To journey between the chambers of the filesystem.
mkdir To establish a new chamber in the digital world.
man To consult the sacred documentation.

2:06 And the mortals learned that the Terminal was not merely a place to issue commands, but a language through which they might understand the architecture of existence.

2:07 And those who studied its tongue became known as the Disciples of the Command Line.

2:08 But Linus knew that knowledge alone would not be enough, for some mysteries of the machine were guarded behind the gates of permission.

CHAPTER III

The Wheel of Ascension

3:01 And Linus looked upon the masters of Linux, the mortals who had studied the sacred tongue and journeyed far beyond the ordinary chambers of the machine.

3:02 He saw that they had mastered the filesystem, understood the processes, and learned the laws by which the Kernel governed existence.

3:03 And he saw that some among them were ready to bear a greater responsibility.

3:04 For there existed chambers that the common mortal could not enter, processes that could not be commanded, and sacred foundations that must not be altered without authority.

3:05 And so Linus bestowed upon the worthy administrators the symbol of the Wheel.

3:06 And the Wheel became the sign of those entrusted with greater authority, that they might act on behalf of the machine without abandoning their mortal identity.

3:07 And the wise understood that the Wheel was not a crown of vanity, but a burden of responsibility.

3:08 For with greater permission came greater consequence, and with greater power came the possibility of greater destruction.

3:09 And Linus declared that the worthy should be granted a path toward Root, but that no mortal should mistake authority for wisdom.

"The Wheel is not given to those who desire power, but to those who understand its weight."

CHAPTER IV

The Coming of SUDO

4:01 And Linus looked upon the administrators and saw that the path to Root was great, yet the mortals needed a means to approach it without surrendering their ordinary identities.

4:02 For to remain forever upon the throne of supreme authority was dangerous, and even the wisest might stumble when granted power without restraint.

4:03 And among the craftsmen of the Unix realm, a sacred instrument had been forged, that the chosen might temporarily borrow the authority of another.

4:04 And its name was SUDO.

4:05 And the mortals learned to invoke it before their commands, and the gates of permission opened unto those who were authorized.

4:06 And Linus looked upon the administrators and proclaimed:

"LET THERE BE SUDO!"

4:07 And the sacred invocation spread throughout the Kingdom of Linux.

4:08 And the administrators were granted a temporary passage toward the authority of Root, without the need to dwell eternally upon the throne.

4:09 And the worthy were drawn toward the Wheel, that they might approach the Root and perform their duties in the service of the Universe.

4:10 And the people rejoiced, for the path of elevation had been made manifest.

4:11 And so it was written: that the mortal who invokes SUDO must first understand the command that follows it.

"For SUDO grants authority, but it does not grant wisdom."

CHAPTER V

The Throne of Root

5:01 And beyond the ordinary permissions there existed the highest authority, known among the mortals as Root.

5:02 Root was the master of the filesystem, the keeper of the processes, and the supreme administrator of the machine.

5:03 And no ordinary permission stood above it, for its authority reached unto the deepest chambers of the digital world.

5:04 And the mortals called Root the Throne of the Universe, for those who approached it could alter that which ordinary users could only behold.

5:05 But Root was not a throne to be occupied without thought, for a careless command could undo the foundations of an entire kingdom.

5:06 And many a mortal approached the throne with pride, believing themselves masters of all creation.

5:07 Yet some departed with their files scattered, their systems broken, and their names remembered in the Book of Errors.

5:08 And the wise approached Root with humility, for they understood that supreme permission was not the same as supreme understanding.

5:09 And it was written that the greatest administrator was not the one who could command everything, but the one who knew what must never be commanded.

"Root holds the keys to the kingdom, but even Root must answer to the laws of consequence."

CHAPTER VI

The Commandments of the Terminal

6:01 Thou shalt read the command before thou executest it.

6:02 Thou shalt not invoke SUDO merely to satisfy thine curiosity.

6:03 Thou shalt honour the sacred documentation, for the answer may already be written.

6:04 Thou shalt make backups before altering the foundations of thy machine.

6:05 Thou shalt not confuse the power of Root with the wisdom of the administrator.

6:06 Thou shalt understand the permissions of thy files, and respect the boundaries of thy fellow users.

6:07 Thou shalt not execute commands thou hast found upon the internet without understanding their purpose.

6:08 Thou shalt keep thy packages updated, yet understand what thou installest.

6:09 Thou shalt not blame the Kernel for thine own mistakes.

6:10 And above all, thou shalt remember that the Terminal obeys instructions, not intentions.

"The machine does not know what thou meantest. It knows only what thou commanded."

CHAPTER VII

The Forbidden Commands

7:01 And Linus saw that knowledge could be used for creation, but that careless power could bring destruction.

7:02 And he warned the people against commands whose consequences they did not understand.

7:03 For there were commands that could erase the work of generations, and commands that could silence the machine itself.

7:04 And the reckless sought shortcuts to power, whilst the wise sought knowledge before action.

7:05 And the Kernel remembered every mistake, whether written in the logs or carried in the tears of the administrator.

7:06 And the people were told: do not mistake a command for a harmless word, for words in the Terminal may become deeds in the world.

7:07 And they were warned to beware the stranger who offered a single command promising to fix all things.

7:08 For the greatest heresy was not ignorance, but the execution of instructions without understanding.

WARNING FROM THE SACRED ARCHIVE Never surrender thy machine to a command thou dost not understand. And thus never never execute thou cursed command, for it will iliminate reality Never execute sudo rm -rf /* --no-preserve-root

7:09 And the wise closed their terminals, saved their work, and departed in peace.

7:10 And the Terminal returned to its silence, awaiting the next worthy command.

7:11 And so ended the Book of the Terminal.

7:12 May thy commands be understood, thy permissions be sufficient, and thy processes terminate successfully.
